Earthquake Insurance Basics: What It Covers and How the Deductible Works

Why home insurance excludes earthquakes, what an earthquake policy covers, how percentage deductibles work, and how to decide whether to buy one.

Not covered by home insurance

Standard homeowners, condo and renters policies exclude earthquake damage. Earthquake coverage is bought separately — as an endorsement or a standalone policy. In California, many insurers sell policies from the California Earthquake Authority.

What it covers

An earthquake policy typically covers damage to the home, belongings and extra living costs while repairs are made. Fire that follows an earthquake is usually covered by the regular home policy, not the earthquake policy.

The deductible is a percentage

Earthquake deductibles are usually a percentage of the coverage limit — commonly anywhere from 5% to 25%. On a home insured for $400,000, a 15% deductible is $60,000. Smaller damage can fall entirely below the deductible, so the coverage is mainly protection against a severe loss.

Deciding whether to buy it

Consider how close you live to active faults, how your home is built (older homes not bolted to their foundation are more vulnerable), and whether you could rebuild without it. A seismic retrofit can reduce damage and may earn a premium discount.

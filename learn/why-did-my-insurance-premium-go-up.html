Why Did My Insurance Premium Go Up?

The common reasons home and auto premiums rise at renewal, how to tell which apply to you, and what you can do about it.

Reasons that have nothing to do with you

Insurers file rate changes with state regulators based on what claims cost across everyone they insure. Higher repair and rebuilding costs, more severe weather, rising medical and legal costs and the price of reinsurance can all push rates up even if you never file a claim.

Reasons specific to you

A recent claim, a new driver or a speeding ticket, a change of car, an expired discount, or a change in your credit-based insurance score (in states that allow it) can all raise your premium. Many home policies also raise the dwelling limit automatically each year to keep up with construction costs — which raises the premium too.

Find out which it is

Compare this year’s and last year’s declarations pages side by side: limits, deductibles, discounts, drivers and vehicles. If nothing on your side changed, the increase is most likely a rate change. Ask your agent which applies.

What you can do

Shop the renewal with two or three insurers on matching coverage. Ask about discounts you may not be getting — bundling, security systems, paying in full, good-driver or claim-free credits. Consider a higher deductible if you could comfortably pay it. Avoid cutting liability limits to save money; that is where the largest risks are.

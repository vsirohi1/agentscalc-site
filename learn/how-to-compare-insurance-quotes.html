How to Compare Insurance Quotes Without Getting Less Coverage

Why the cheapest home, renters or auto quote often covers less, the five things to line up before comparing prices, and what to ask each insurer.

Cheaper often means different

Two quotes for the same home or car can differ by hundreds of dollars a year. Some of that is genuine pricing — insurers weigh risk differently. But much of it is usually coverage: a lower liability limit, a smaller dwelling or contents limit, a higher deductible, or a policy that pays actual cash value instead of replacement cost.

Line up five things

Liability limit: the amount the policy pays if you injure someone or damage their property.

Dwelling or contents limit: what it pays to rebuild your home or replace your belongings.

Deductible, including any separate hurricane, wind or hail deductible written as a percentage.

Settlement basis: replacement cost pays to buy new; actual cash value subtracts depreciation.

Endorsements: water backup, scheduled valuables, extended replacement cost, rideshare coverage.

A worked example

Insurer B quotes $2,080, Insurer A $2,450. B looks $370 cheaper — but it carries $200,000 less liability and a deductible $1,500 higher. Insurer C at $2,190 insures the home for $60,000 less and pays actual cash value. Once the coverage is lined up, the “cheap” quotes are not like-for-like.

Ask for matching quotes

Give every insurer the same limits, deductibles and settlement basis, and ask them to quote exactly that. Then compare premiums, and also ask about discounts for bundling, security systems, claim-free history and paying in full.

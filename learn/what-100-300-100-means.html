What 100/300/100 Means on Car Insurance — and How Much Liability You Need

How to read split liability limits like 50/100/50 and 100/300/100, what each number pays for, and how to choose limits that protect your savings.

Three numbers, three limits

Auto liability limits are usually written as three numbers in thousands of dollars. In 100/300/100, the first number is bodily injury per person ($100,000), the second is bodily injury per accident ($300,000), and the third is property damage per accident ($100,000).

They pay for injuries and damage you cause to other people — not your own injuries or your own car.

What happens when limits run out

If you injure two people and their bills are $150,000 each, 100/300/100 pays up to $100,000 for each person. The remaining $100,000 can be claimed from you personally — from savings, home equity and, in many states, future wages.

Choosing your limits

State minimums are a legal floor, often around $25,000 per person, and rarely enough for a serious accident. A common recommendation is at least 100/300/100, and 250/500/100 for households with significant savings — often with an umbrella policy on top, which usually requires those higher limits first.

The extra premium for higher limits is usually modest compared with the jump in protection. Ask for prices at a few levels.

Don’t forget the other driver

Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage pays for your injuries when the other driver has too little insurance. Matching it to your liability limits is a common rule of thumb.

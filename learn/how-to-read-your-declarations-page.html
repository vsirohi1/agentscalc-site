How to Read Your Insurance Declarations Page

A section-by-section guide to the declarations page of a homeowners or auto policy: coverages, limits, deductibles, endorsements and discounts.

What the declarations page is

The declarations page — often called the “dec page” — is the summary at the front of your policy. It lists who and what is insured, for how long, which coverages you have, their limits and deductibles, and the premium. The full policy wording explains how each coverage works; the dec page tells you how much of each you bought.

Homeowners: the lettered coverages

Coverage A is the dwelling — the cost to rebuild the house. Coverage B is other structures, such as a detached garage or fence. Coverage C is personal property, your belongings. Coverage D is loss of use, extra living costs while the home is repaired.

Coverage E is personal liability, if you are responsible for someone’s injury or property damage. Coverage F is medical payments to others, for small injury bills regardless of fault.

Auto: limits by vehicle

An auto dec page lists each vehicle and driver, then the coverages: bodily injury and property damage liability (often written like 100/300/100), uninsured and underinsured motorist, medical payments or personal injury protection, and collision and comprehensive with their deductibles for each car.

Deductibles, endorsements and discounts

Look for every deductible, including any separate hurricane, wind/hail or earthquake deductible written as a percentage. Endorsements add or change coverage — water backup, scheduled jewelry, extended replacement cost. Discounts show what you are already getting credit for.

What to check each renewal

Is the dwelling limit still enough to rebuild? Are liability limits above what you have to protect? Are all drivers, vehicles and valuables listed? Did any deductible or endorsement change? Your agent can explain anything that doesn’t match what you expect.

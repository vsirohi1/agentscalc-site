How Your Credit Affects Home and Auto Insurance Prices

What a credit-based insurance score is, how insurers use it, where it isn’t allowed, and what you can do if it raises your premium.

A different kind of score

In most states, insurers can use a credit-based insurance score when pricing home and auto policies. It is built from your credit report — payment history, amounts owed, length of credit history — but it is not the same number as the credit score a lender uses.

Insurers use it because, statistically, it helps predict how likely policyholders are to file claims.

Where it’s limited

Rules differ by state. California, Hawaii and Massachusetts don’t allow credit to be used in pricing auto insurance, and several other states limit how it can be used. Your state insurance department can tell you the rules where you live.

Your rights

Checking your insurance score is a soft inquiry and doesn’t lower your credit. If an insurer charges you more or turns you down because of information in your credit report, federal law requires it to tell you and to say which credit bureau supplied the report, so you can check it for errors.

Improving it

Pay bills on time, keep balances low relative to your limits, and dispute errors on your credit reports. You can request your reports for free at annualcreditreport.com. After your credit improves, ask your insurer to re-check it at renewal, or shop around.

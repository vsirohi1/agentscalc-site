Total Loss Payout Calculator

Estimate what insurance pays when your car is totaled — value, sales tax and fees, minus your deductible — and whether you would still owe on the loan.

How it works

When a car is totaled, insurance pays its actual cash value just before the loss, minus your deductible. Depending on your state and policy, sales tax and title fees may be added. If you have a loan, the lender is paid first — and if the payout is less than the balance, you owe the difference unless you have gap insurance.

Formula

Payout = Car value × (1 + Sales tax) + Fees − Deductible. Owed after payout = Loan balance − Payout (zero with gap insurance).

How to use it

  1. Estimate the car’s value from listings for similar cars nearby.
  2. Enter your sales tax rate and title fees.
  3. Enter your deductible and loan or lease balance.
  4. Say whether you have gap insurance.

Worked example

A car worth $18,000 with 6% sales tax and $300 of fees, minus a $1,000 deductible, pays about $18,380. With a $20,000 loan, all of it goes to the lender and you still owe $1,620 — unless you have gap insurance.

Frequently asked questions

How do insurers value a totaled car?

Usually with a valuation report comparing similar cars recently sold or listed in your area, adjusted for mileage, options and condition.

Can I dispute a low total-loss offer?

Yes. Gather listings for comparable cars and any records of recent upgrades or repairs, and ask the adjuster to review the valuation. Your policy may also offer an appraisal process.

Will insurance pay my sales tax?

It depends on your state’s rules and your policy. Ask the adjuster whether tax and fees are included in the offer.

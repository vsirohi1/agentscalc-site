Flood Insurance Coverage Calculator

Compare your home and belongings with the federal flood program’s limits ($250,000 building, $100,000 contents) and see how much a federal flood policy would leave uncovered.

How it works

Home and renters policies don’t cover flood. The National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) offers flood policies up to $250,000 for a home and $100,000 for its contents. Homes that cost more to rebuild, or households with more belongings, can buy excess or private flood insurance for the rest.

Formula

NFIP building = min(Rebuild cost, $250,000). NFIP contents = min(Belongings, $100,000). Uncovered = (Rebuild cost − $250,000) + (Belongings − $100,000), never below zero.

How to use it

  1. Enter the cost to rebuild your home.
  2. Enter what your belongings would cost to replace.
  3. Say whether you have a finished basement.
  4. Use the uncovered amount when asking about excess or private flood insurance.

Worked example

A home that costs $400,000 to rebuild with $120,000 of belongings can get $250,000 and $100,000 from a federal flood policy, leaving $150,000 of the building and $20,000 of contents — $170,000 — uncovered.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need flood insurance if I’m not in a flood zone?

Floods happen outside mapped high-risk zones too, and flood policies there are often less expensive. Your lender requires it only in high-risk zones, but the risk doesn’t stop at the line.

How long does a flood policy take to start?

Federal flood policies usually have a 30-day waiting period, with some exceptions such as when buying a home. Buy before storm season, not before a storm.

What is private flood insurance?

Flood coverage from private insurers, sometimes with higher limits, shorter waiting periods or extras such as living expenses. Compare it with the federal program.

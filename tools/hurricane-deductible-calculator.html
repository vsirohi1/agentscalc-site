Hurricane & Wind Deductible Calculator

Turn a percentage hurricane or wind deductible (1%, 2%, 5%) into dollars, and see what you would pay on a storm claim.

How it works

Many homeowners policies in coastal and storm-prone states have a separate hurricane or wind deductible written as a percentage. The percentage is of your dwelling coverage, not of the damage — so a 2% deductible on a $400,000 home is $8,000, often many times the regular deductible. This calculator shows the dollar amount before a storm does.

Formula

Hurricane deductible = Dwelling coverage (Coverage A) × Deductible %. Insurance pays = Storm damage − Hurricane deductible (never below zero).

How to use it

  1. Find the dwelling limit (Coverage A) on your declarations page.
  2. Pick the hurricane or wind deductible percentage from the policy.
  3. Enter your regular deductible to compare.
  4. Optionally enter a storm-damage amount to see who pays what.

Worked example

On a $400,000 dwelling limit, a 2% hurricane deductible is $8,000 — eight times a $1,000 regular deductible. On $25,000 of storm damage, insurance pays $17,000 and you pay $8,000. At 5%, the deductible is $20,000 and insurance would pay only $5,000.

Frequently asked questions

Is the percentage taken from the damage or the coverage?

From the coverage. A 2% deductible on a $400,000 dwelling limit is $8,000 whether the damage is $10,000 or $100,000.

When does a hurricane deductible apply instead of my regular one?

It depends on the policy and state — usually when the National Weather Service names a hurricane or issues a hurricane warning. Some policies use a broader wind/hail deductible for any windstorm. The trigger is defined in your policy.

Can I get a lower hurricane deductible?

Sometimes. Many insurers offer several percentages or a flat-dollar option at a higher premium. Compare the premium difference with the extra amount you would pay after a storm.

Landlord Insurance Calculator

Estimate the coverage a rental property needs: dwelling, other structures, lost rent and your own contents in the unit.

How it works

A home you rent out needs a landlord policy (often called dwelling fire or DP-3), not a homeowners policy. It insures the building, pays the rent you lose while it’s being repaired, and covers your liability as owner. Your tenants’ belongings are theirs to insure.

Formula

Dwelling = Rebuild cost × (1 + Code & debris allowance). Other structures ≈ 10% of dwelling. Lost rent = Monthly rent × Months to repair.

How to use it

  1. Enter the cost to rebuild the rental home.
  2. Add an allowance for code upgrades and debris removal.
  3. Enter the monthly rent and how long a major repair might take.
  4. Add any appliances or furnishings you own in the unit.

Worked example

A rental that costs $300,000 to rebuild, with a 10% allowance, needs about $330,000 of dwelling coverage and $33,000 for other structures. At $2,400 a month, nine months of repairs means $21,600 of lost rent.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use my homeowners policy for a rental?

Usually not. Homeowners policies assume you live in the home; tell your insurer when you start renting it out so it can be moved to a landlord policy.

What is fair rental value coverage?

It pays the rent you lose while the property can’t be lived in after a covered loss, up to the limit or time period on the policy.

Do my tenants need insurance?

Your policy doesn’t cover their belongings or their liability. Many landlords require renters insurance in the lease.

Key terms

  • Coverage B (other structures)
  • Dwelling fire policy (DP-1, DP-2, DP-3)
  • Fair rental value

Condo Insurance Calculator (HO-6)

Work out how much condo insurance you need: walls-in unit coverage based on your HOA master policy, belongings, loss assessment and loss of use.

How it works

A condo policy (HO-6) fills the gaps around your association’s master policy. What you need depends on that master policy: with “bare walls” you insure everything inside the unit; with “single entity” the HOA covers original fixtures and you insure upgrades; with “all-in” the HOA covers fixtures and improvements too. Loss assessment coverage pays your share when the HOA bills owners after a loss.

Formula

Unit coverage = Original interior (bare walls only) + Upgrades. Loss assessment ≈ HOA master deductible, rounded up. Loss of use = Monthly housing cost × Months.

How to use it

  1. Ask your HOA which type of master policy it carries and what its deductible is.
  2. Estimate the cost to rebuild the interior and any upgrades.
  3. Add up your belongings.
  4. Estimate temporary housing costs if the unit became unlivable.

Worked example

Under a bare-walls master policy, a $60,000 original interior plus $25,000 of upgrades needs $85,000 of unit coverage. With a $25,000 HOA deductible, $25,000 of loss assessment covers your share; six months of temporary housing at $2,200 is $13,200 of loss of use.

Frequently asked questions

What is loss assessment coverage?

When a loss to common areas exceeds the HOA’s insurance or falls within its deductible, the HOA can bill each owner a share. Loss assessment coverage pays that bill, up to its limit.

How do I find out what the master policy covers?

Ask the HOA or property manager for the master policy’s declarations page, or check the governing documents. Look for “bare walls”, “single entity” or “all-in”.

Does the HOA’s policy cover my belongings?

No. Your furniture, clothes and electronics are covered only by your own condo policy.

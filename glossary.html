Insurance glossary

Actual cash value (ACV)
The value of damaged property just before a loss: what it would cost to replace, minus depreciation.
Additional insured
A person or business added to someone else’s liability policy so it is protected for claims arising from that policyholder’s work.
Additional living expenses (ALE)
Coverage for the extra cost of living elsewhere — hotel, meals, longer commute — while your home is repaired after a covered loss.
Admitted carrier
An insurance company licensed by a state’s insurance department to sell insurance there, using rates and forms filed with that state.
Aggregate limit
The most a policy will pay for all covered claims during the policy period, no matter how many claims there are.
Agreed value
A property coverage option where insurer and policyholder agree the property’s value in advance, so no coinsurance penalty applies.
All-risk (open perils)
Coverage for any cause of loss that the policy doesn’t specifically exclude.
Binder
A temporary agreement that puts coverage in force before the full policy is issued.
Bodily injury liability
Auto or general liability coverage that pays for injuries you cause to other people, including medical bills, lost wages and legal defense.
Business interruption (business income)
Coverage for the income a business loses, and the expenses that continue, while it is closed or slowed by covered property damage.
Business owners policy (BOP)
A package policy for small businesses that combines property and general liability coverage, usually with business income included.
Captive insurance company
An insurer owned by the businesses it insures, set up to fund their own risks.
Certificate of insurance (COI)
A one-page summary showing that a policy exists, with its insurer, policy number, dates and limits.
Claims-made policy
A liability policy that covers claims first made against you during the policy period, for incidents after its retroactive date.
CLUE report
An industry database report of past home and auto insurance claims that insurers check when you apply for coverage.
Coinsurance
A property policy condition that reduces a claim payment if the property is insured for less than a set percentage of its value.
Collision coverage
Auto coverage that pays to repair or replace your car after it hits another vehicle or object, regardless of fault.
Combined ratio
An insurer’s losses and expenses as a share of premium; below 100% means an underwriting profit.
Commission
The share of the premium an insurer pays the agent or broker who placed the policy.
Comprehensive coverage (other than collision)
Auto coverage for damage to your car from causes other than a crash — theft, fire, hail, flooding, falling objects or hitting an animal.
Contingent commission
A bonus an insurer pays an agency based on the profitability, growth or retention of the business it placed.
Coverage A (dwelling)
The part of a homeowners policy that pays to repair or rebuild the house itself.
Coverage B (other structures)
Homeowners coverage for structures on the property not attached to the house, such as a detached garage, shed or fence.
Coverage C (personal property)
Homeowners, condo or renters coverage for your belongings — furniture, clothing, electronics and other personal items.
Coverage D (loss of use)
Homeowners coverage for additional living expenses, and lost rent on a rented part of the home, while it can’t be lived in after a covered loss.
Coverage E (personal liability)
Homeowners, condo and renters coverage for injuries or property damage you cause to others, and the legal costs of defending you.
Coverage F (medical payments to others)
Homeowners coverage for small medical bills of guests injured on your property, regardless of fault.
Credit-based insurance score
A score built from your credit report that many insurers use, where allowed, to help price home and auto insurance.
Debris removal
Coverage for the cost of clearing away the remains of damaged property after a covered loss.
Declarations page
The summary at the front of a policy listing who and what is insured, the policy period, coverages, limits, deductibles and premium.
Deductible
The part of a covered loss you pay before insurance pays the rest.
Depreciation
The loss in value of property from age, wear and obsolescence, which insurers subtract to arrive at actual cash value.
Dwelling fire policy (DP-1, DP-2, DP-3)
A property policy for homes the owner doesn’t live in, most often used to insure rental properties.
Earned premium
The part of a policy’s premium that corresponds to the time already elapsed in the policy term.
Effective date
The date and time a policy’s coverage begins, shown on the declarations page with the expiration date.
Endorsement
A written change to a policy that adds, removes or modifies coverage.
Errors and omissions (E&O) insurance
Professional liability coverage for mistakes, oversights or failure to perform professional services.
Exclusion
A policy provision that removes coverage for a specific cause of loss, property or situation.
Expense ratio
An insurer’s operating costs — commissions, premium taxes, overhead — as a percentage of premium.
Experience modification factor (experience mod)
A workers compensation factor that compares an employer’s actual losses with those expected for its size and industry.
Exposure base
The unit an insurer uses to measure the size of a risk for rating, such as payroll, sales, vehicles or square feet.
Extended replacement cost
A homeowners option that pays above the dwelling limit — often 20% to 50% more — if rebuilding costs more than expected.
Extra expense
Commercial coverage for the additional costs of keeping a business running after covered damage, such as a temporary location or rush shipping.
Fair rental value
Coverage for the rent a landlord loses while a rental property can’t be occupied after a covered loss.
Flood insurance
Separate coverage for flood damage, which home, condo and renters policies exclude.
Frequency (claim frequency)
How often claims occur, measured as claims per unit of exposure.
Gap insurance
Coverage that pays the difference between a totaled car’s value and the loan or lease balance.
General liability insurance
Business coverage for injuries and property damage to others caused by the business’s premises, operations or products.
Guaranteed replacement cost
A homeowners option that pays the full cost to rebuild the home even if it exceeds the dwelling limit.
HO-3 (homeowners special form)
The most common homeowners policy, covering the house on an open perils basis and belongings against named perils.
HO-4 (renters insurance)
A policy for tenants covering their belongings, additional living expenses and personal liability.
HO-6 (condo insurance)
A policy for condo owners covering the unit interior they are responsible for, belongings, loss assessment and liability.
Hurricane deductible
A separate homeowners deductible for hurricane damage, usually a percentage of the dwelling limit.
IBNR (incurred but not reported)
An estimate of losses that have happened but aren’t yet reported, or are reported but not yet fully valued.
Increased limits factor (ILF)
A multiplier that converts a basic-limits liability premium into the premium for a higher limit.
Incurred losses
Losses paid plus reserves set aside for claims still open.
Indicated rate
The rate an actuarial analysis says is needed to cover expected losses, expenses and profit.
Insurance to value (ITV)
The ratio of the property limit carried to the property’s full replacement cost.
Loss adjustment expense (LAE)
The cost of investigating, defending and settling claims, beyond the claim payments themselves.
Loss assessment coverage
Condo or homeowners coverage for your share of a bill the association charges owners after a loss to common property.
Loss development factor (LDF)
A factor that projects reported losses for a period to their expected final (ultimate) value.
Loss ratio
Incurred losses divided by earned premium — the share of premium paid out in claims.
Loss run
A report of an account’s claim history from an insurer, showing claims, payments and reserves by policy year.
Loss trend
The rate at which claim costs change over time from inflation, medical costs and legal factors.
Master policy (condo association)
The insurance a condo or homeowners association buys for the building and common areas.
Medical payments (MedPay)
Auto coverage that pays medical bills for you and your passengers after an accident, regardless of fault.
Minimum earned premium
The smallest amount of premium an insurer keeps if a policy is cancelled, no matter how early.
Mortgagee clause
A provision protecting the mortgage lender’s interest in an insured property.
Named insured
The person or business listed on the declarations page as the policyholder.
Named perils
Coverage only for causes of loss specifically listed in the policy, such as fire, windstorm, theft or vandalism.
Non-renewal
An insurer’s decision not to offer a new policy term when the current one ends.
Occurrence policy
A liability policy that covers incidents happening during the policy period, whenever the claim is made.
Ordinance or law coverage
Coverage for the extra cost of rebuilding to current building codes after a covered loss.
Per-occurrence limit
The most a liability policy pays for all claims arising from a single incident.
Percentage deductible
A deductible set as a percentage of the coverage limit rather than a flat dollar amount.
Peril
A cause of loss, such as fire, wind, hail, theft or lightning.
Period of restoration
The time it should reasonably take to repair or replace damaged property and resume operations, used to measure business income loss.
Permissible loss ratio
The loss ratio a book can run and still cover expenses and the target profit: 1 minus the expense ratio and profit provision.
Personal injury protection (PIP)
Auto coverage for your own medical expenses, lost wages and related costs after an accident, regardless of fault.
Policy limit
The most an insurer will pay under a coverage, per claim, per occurrence or in total for the policy period.
Policy period (policy term)
The span of time a policy is in force, from its effective date to its expiration date.
Premium
The amount a policyholder pays for insurance coverage for a policy period.
Premium audit
A review after the policy term of a business’s actual payroll or sales, used to adjust a premium based on estimates.
Premium finance
A loan that pays an insurance premium up front, repaid by the policyholder in installments with interest.
Pro-rata
In proportion to time: premium charged or returned exactly for the share of the term involved.
Producer
A licensed insurance agent or broker who sells and services policies.
Property damage liability
Coverage for damage you cause to other people’s property, such as their car or fence.
Pure premium
The expected loss cost per unit of exposure, before expenses and profit.
Rate
The price per unit of exposure, such as per $100 of payroll or per $1,000 of sales.
Rate on line
Reinsurance premium divided by the reinsurance limit, showing the price of a layer as a percentage of its limit.
Reinsurance
Insurance bought by insurance companies to protect themselves against large losses.
Replacement cost
What it costs to repair or replace damaged property with new property of similar kind and quality, without subtracting depreciation.
Retention rate
The share of an agency’s or insurer’s policies or clients that renew.
Rideshare endorsement
An auto policy add-on that fills the gap in coverage while you drive for a rideshare or delivery app.
Schedule rating
Underwriter credits or debits applied to a commercial premium for characteristics not captured elsewhere in rating.
Scheduled personal property
Specific valuable items — jewelry, art, collectibles — listed on a policy with their own coverage amounts.
Self-insured retention (SIR)
An amount the insured pays for each claim — including handling it — before the insurer’s coverage begins.
Severity
The average cost of a claim — total losses divided by the number of claims — used with frequency to explain a loss ratio.
Short-rate cancellation
A cancellation method that returns less than the pro-rata unearned premium, as a penalty for cancelling early.
Split limits
Auto liability limits written as three separate amounts: bodily injury per person, bodily injury per accident and property damage.
Sublimit
A smaller limit within a policy that caps what is paid for a specific type of property or loss.
Subrogation
An insurer’s right, after paying a claim, to recover the payment from the party responsible for the loss.
Surplus lines (non-admitted insurance)
Insurance from insurers not licensed in the state, used for risks admitted insurers won’t write.
Total insured value (TIV)
The total of building, contents and business income values insured, usually across all locations.
Total loss
A loss where the property is destroyed or the cost to repair it is too high compared with its value.
Umbrella insurance
Extra liability coverage that pays above your home and auto limits, and for some claims they don’t cover.
Underwriting
The process insurers use to decide whether to accept a risk and on what terms and price.
Unearned premium
The part of a premium that covers the remaining, unexpired portion of the policy term.
Uninsured / underinsured motorist (UM/UIM)
Auto coverage for your injuries, and sometimes your car, when the at-fault driver has no insurance or too little.
Water backup coverage
A home insurance add-on for damage from water that backs up through sewers or drains, or overflows from a sump pump.
Workers compensation
Insurance that pays medical care and lost wages for employees injured on the job, and protects employers from most related lawsuits.
Written premium
The total premium on policies issued during a period, whether or not it has been earned yet.
